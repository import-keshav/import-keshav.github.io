DeepSeek, GLM & Kimi: How Chinese AI Models Are Catching Up to Claude and OpenAI

22 August, 2026 · ~9 min read

At the start of 2025, “frontier AI” mostly meant two American labs: OpenAI and Anthropic. ChatGPT was where the mainstream went. Claude was becoming the favourite for serious coding and long agent workflows.

OpenAI shipped reasoning models such as o1 and o3, then brought reasoning, coding and tool use together in the GPT-5 era. Anthropic moved from Claude 3.7 to Claude 4 and made Claude Code a practical terminal agent. For companies, these products felt like the safe frontier: polished apps, familiar APIs, strong developer tools and enterprise trust.

By August 2026, the picture looked different. Chinese labs — DeepSeek, Zhipu AI (GLM), and Moonshot AI (Kimi) — were no longer just making cheaper alternatives. They were getting close to the top models on public evaluations while costing much less to use. The interesting story is not only that they caught up. It is how quickly they did it.

2025: the year OpenAI and Claude set the pace

The US labs established the product categories everyone else had to chase:

  • Reasoning — OpenAI’s o-series made “let the model think longer” a mainstream feature.
  • Coding — GPT-4.1, Claude 3.7 and Claude 4 made repo-level software work much more useful.
  • Agents — tool use and Claude Code showed that a model could do work, not only answer questions.
  • Distribution — ChatGPT, Claude, IDE integrations and enterprise contracts put US models in front of millions of users.

The lead was not just about benchmark scores. It was model quality plus product, distribution and trust. That is a much harder lead to attack.

The turning point: strong models became cheap and open

The shift actually started at the end of 2024. DeepSeek-V3 used a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) design: the model had 671 billion total parameters, but activated only about 37 billion for each token. Instead of using the whole brain for every word, it called only the specialist parts needed for that moment.

Then DeepSeek-R1 arrived in January 2025 with reinforcement-learning-based reasoning, downloadable weights, and aggressive API pricing. Suddenly “open and cheap” did not have to mean “far behind.”

By July 2025, Kimi K2 and GLM-4.5 applied the same broad playbook to coding and agents. Through 2026, these families kept iterating on multimodality, longer context and agentic tool use.

How did they catch up so quickly?

There was no single magic trick. The public papers and releases point to a stack of engineering choices:

  • Sparse MoE architectures — huge total capacity, but only a small part runs for each token. This lowers inference cost.
  • More efficient attention and training — techniques such as latent or sparse attention, FP8 training and better communication between GPUs reduced wasted compute.
  • Reinforcement learning and distillation — strong reasoning behaviour was learned, then transferred into smaller or more practical models.
  • Open weights — developers could download, fine-tune and deploy models, creating adoption much faster than a closed waitlist.
  • Aggressive API pricing — low prices gave startups and agent builders a reason to test these models in production.
  • Fast iteration — DeepSeek V3 → R1, Kimi K2 → K2.5, and GLM’s open releases compressed years of catch-up into months.

Industry observers also argue that limited access to the newest US chips pushed Chinese labs toward efficiency. That is a reasonable interpretation, not something one benchmark can prove. What the technical reports do prove is that these teams became extremely good at getting more from each unit of compute.

2026: catch-up became competition

In 2025, the surprise was that Chinese open models could get close. In 2026, the surprise was the pace: larger context windows, better coding agents, multimodal inputs and new price cuts arrived within months of each other. The conversation moved from “can they match a US model?” to “which model gives me the best result for this workload and budget?”

That distinction matters. A startup running millions of agent turns may prefer a model that is 95% as good for one-fifth the cost. A company with sensitive data may prefer open weights it can host itself. A developer working across a huge repository may care more about context length than one extra benchmark point.

DeepSeek (DeepSeek / 深度求索)

Site: deepseek.com · API docs: api-docs.deepseek.com

DeepSeek became famous for a simple idea: strong reasoning and coding quality at a price that feels almost unfair. Models like DeepSeek-V3 and the reasoning-focused DeepSeek-R1 helped set the “open / cheap frontier” narrative. Newer API models (V4-class Flash / Pro) keep pushing long context and thinking modes.

What stands out:

  • Low cost — API rates that undercut typical Claude / GPT frontier pricing by a wide margin.
  • Fast enough for real work — especially Flash-style SKUs for everyday coding and chat.
  • Strong reasoning / coding value — good at math and software tasks relative to what you pay.
  • Developer-friendly APIs — easy to plug into tools that already speak OpenAI-style endpoints.

GLM (Zhipu AI / 智谱 · Z.ai)

Company: Zhipu AI · Product / research: z.ai · GLM-5 announcement: z.ai/blog/glm-5

GLM is Zhipu’s model family. With GLM-5 (and the earlier GLM-4.5 / 4.7 line), they positioned open-weight models as serious options for coding agents, long-horizon tasks, and document-heavy workflows — not just demos.

What stands out:

  • Open weights mindset — more room to self-host, fine-tune, and audit.
  • Agentic coding story — aimed at long coding sessions, not only one-shot answers.
  • Practical deliverables — office / document style agent outputs in their product narrative.
  • Cost + deploy flexibility — useful when you care about infra choice, not only a hosted chat box.

Kimi (Moonshot AI / 月之暗面)

Company: Moonshot AI · Chat: kimi.com · API: platform.kimi.ai

Kimi’s brand is “bring the whole context.” From Kimi K2 into K2.5 (multimodal / agentic) and later K3-class flagships with very large context windows, Moonshot leaned into long documents, big codebases, and agent workflows.

What stands out:

  • Huge context — strong for whole-repo and long-doc jobs where smaller windows choke.
  • Agentic + multimodal — tools, vision, and multi-step work in one product story.
  • Coding-focused SKUs — dedicated “code” variants for software engineering loops.
  • Choice of tiers — cheaper K2.x options for volume, premium flagship when you need max quality.

The gap is now smaller than the price gap

These Artificial Analysis screenshots are a useful point-in-time snapshot. Their Intelligence Index combines several evaluations; it is not a universal measure of which model is “best.”

The US models still lead this chart: Claude sits at the top and GPT is close. But Kimi is only a few points behind, while DeepSeek and GLM remain in the competitive group. That would already be notable. The cost chart makes it much more important.

In this snapshot, DeepSeek V4 Pro costs about $0.25 per task and GLM-5 about $0.32. GPT is around $1.23, while the Claude entries are roughly $2.34–$3.14. The honest conclusion is not “Chinese models beat everything.” It is: the price of near-frontier intelligence has collapsed.

How the competition actually feels

Put simply:

  • DeepSeek — “why am I paying 10× for this task?”
  • GLM — “can I own / host a near-frontier model?”
  • Kimi — “can I stuff the whole problem into context and still move fast?”
  • Claude / GPT — still the default when you want maximum polish, trust, and product ecosystem.

Nobody “won AI.” What changed is that builders now have a real menu — not a two-company monopoly of taste.

Why this race is good for end users

Geopolitics makes the headlines. Users feel something more practical:

  • Lower prices — pressure from cheap Chinese APIs forces everyone to ship better value tiers.
  • Faster shipping — more labs shipping means more experiments, more model drops, less waiting.
  • Open options — open weights mean privacy, custom fine-tunes, and self-hosting when you need them.
  • Real choice — pick cheap reasoning today, long-context tomorrow, Claude when the task is delicate.

Healthy competition is not noise. It is why frontier intelligence is spreading beyond a small club — into tools, startups, and everyday developer workflows.

TL;DR

In August 2026, Chinese models from DeepSeek, Zhipu (GLM), and Moonshot (Kimi) are competing hard with OpenAI and Anthropic Claude — especially on cost, open weights, coding agents, and long context. US models still set a high bar. The win for the rest of us is simple: better models, more choice, and lower bills.
